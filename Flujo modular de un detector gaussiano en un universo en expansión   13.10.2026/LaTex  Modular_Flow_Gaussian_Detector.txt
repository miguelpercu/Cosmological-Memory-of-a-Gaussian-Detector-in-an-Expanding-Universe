\begin{abstract}
We present a 3+1D Wightman-based memory analysis of a Gaussian detector
coupled to a scalar field through a matter-dominated expansion history.
The calculation is framed via the modular flow of Tomita--Takesaki and
the quantum Fisher information $F_Q$ of the detector's reduced state,
testing the intuition of the Universal Applied Time (UAT) framework
that time is a measure of causal connection.

Three results are reported. First, the Unruh--DeWitt consistency check
(KMS condition) passes at machine precision ($\max|\Delta W/W|
\lesssim 10^{-14}$ for accelerations $a \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$), which
validates the numerical apparatus. Second, the maximum information
mutual $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ between detector and field converges with
$N$ to approximately $0.26$ nats, while the power-law exponent $\alpha$
of the Fisher-information decay does \emph{not} converge with $N$.
We show that this non-convergence is a physical consequence of the
finite-dimensional unitary dynamics (quasi-periodic orbits, no genuine
decay), not a numerical artefact.

Third, we compare two continuum models. A \emph{static-bath} quantum
Brownian motion model with Ohmic spectral density gives $\Gamma =
\pi\eta/2$, independent of temperature and cutoff. However, an
\emph{exact} treatment using the Wightman function of a scalar field
in FRW shows that the effective damping rate $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ decays
with the expansion and exhibits memory effects (sign-changing
oscillations, associated with particle creation) in the matter and
dark-energy regimes. The global decay is robust against the choice of
UV regulator; the precise location of the sign change is
scheme-dependent. The Ohmic closure is mathematically exact but
describes the wrong system: a detector coupled to an external static
bath, not to a cosmological field.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Universal Applied Time (UAT) framework---also referred to by its
Spanish acronym TAU, \emph{Tiempo Aplicable Unificado}---was developed
independently by the author during 2025 and proposed that the time
assigned to an event is a measure of the causal connection between
that event and an observer. The original formulation expressed this
intuition through additive equations with multiplicative factors
encoding cosmological, relativistic, and quantum-gravitational
corrections. Systematic tests against DESI DR2 baryon acoustic
oscillation data and Pantheon supernova data established that the
additive formalism was not competitive with $\Lambda$CDM, and
subsequent analysis identified the root cause as a structural
incompatibility between the relationalist intuition and the additive
formalism \citep{Percudani2026}.

The present paper does not attempt to salvage the original equations.
Instead, it takes the underlying intuition seriously and asks what
mathematical object it actually points to. The answer---developed in
collaboration with automated reasoning tools and formalised in a
companion specification---is that the intuition reduces, in the
calculable regime, to a statement about the \emph{informational cost}
of maintaining temporal resolution in an expanding universe.

It is well established that the Markovian approximation for quantum
detectors fails in curved spacetimes
\citep{LoukoSatz2006,Fukuma2014,Burgess2020,Waghmare2025}, and that
non-Markovianity and memory effects are standard features of open
quantum systems \citep{Breuer2009,Breuer2016}. In particular,
\citet{HsiangHu2021} have shown that an Unruh--DeWitt detector with
harmonic oscillator internal degrees of freedom, coupled to a quantum
field in an expanding universe, exhibits non-Markovian memory effects
associated with the squeezing of the field by the cosmic dynamics. The
contribution of the present work is not to claim the first
demonstration of non-Markovianity in cosmology. It is to provide a
specific 3+1D Gaussian-detector calculation across a full
matter-dominated expansion history, framed via the quantum Fisher
information and the modular flow, and to compare explicitly against
the Ohmic quantum Brownian motion closure, showing that the latter is
exact for the wrong system.

\subsection{Relation to the modular program}
\label{sec:relation}

The technical framework of this paper is the modular theory of
Tomita--Takesaki \citep{Connes1973, Takesaki1970}, as applied to
quantum clocks in cosmological settings by recent work
\citep{Chandrasekaran2022, Chen2024, Chen2026, Speranza2025,
DeVuyst2025, Gomez2022, Espindola2026}.

The UAT/TAU framework was developed independently by the author in
2025, from a starting point in cosmological numerical modeling and
direct communication with experimental collaborations. Its public
development record is documented in Section~\ref{sec:records}. For
the reader's reference, the following dates are relevant:

\begin{itemize}
\item Chen and Penington formalised the modular treatment of
cosmological clocks in June 2024 \citep{Chen2024}.
\item De Vuyst and collaborators developed the crossed-product and
quantum-reference-frame formulation of gravitational entropy in
December 2024 \citep{DeVuyst2025}.
\item Speranza proposed the intrinsic cosmological observer in
April 2025 \citep{Speranza2025}.
\item The UAT/TAU framework was released publicly on GitHub in June
2025 and on Zenodo in November 2025 (Section~\ref{sec:records}).
\item Chen and collaborators extended the crossed-product approach
to covariant observers in de Sitter space in November 2025
\citep{Chen2026}.
\end{itemize}

The convergence of UAT with the modular program is therefore
independent and parallel. The framework does not claim priority over
\citet{Chen2024, DeVuyst2025}, whose work predates it. What UAT
contributes is a phenomenological intuition developed outside the
algebraic quantum field theory community, from a starting point in
cosmological numerical modeling. The present paper takes that
intuition, translates it into the language of modular theory, and
provides its first quantitative test.

\section{What UAT/TAU Really Points To}
\label{sec:core}

Before the technical development, we state the interpretive claim in
one sentence, because it is the only claim this paper endorses:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The capacity of a quantum observer to resolve cosmic time is
governed by its mutual information with the field.}
\end{quote}

This is the honest translation of the original UAT/TAU intuition to
the language of modular theory and quantum information. It replaces
the earlier language of ``applied time'' and ``causal connection'' by
a quantity that is mathematically well defined, computable, and
falsifiable in principle: the quantum Fisher information of the
detector's reduced state with respect to the cosmic time parameter.

No stronger claim is made in this paper. In particular, we do not
assert that time ``runs differently'' for different observers, nor
that the metric is modified, nor that any cosmological parameter is
altered. The observable is the resolution of a clock, not the rate of
time.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Background and field}

We work in a spatially flat FRW universe with line element
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = -dt^2 + a^2(t)\,d\vec{x}^2,
\end{equation}
and adopt the matter-dominated regime $a(\eta) = \eta^2$ in
conformal time $\eta$, which is sufficient for the numerical
demonstration. A real massive scalar field $\phi$ of mass
$m_\phi \ll H_0$ is expanded in comoving modes,
\begin{equation}
\phi(\vec{x}, \eta) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L^3}} \sum_{\vec{k}}
\left[ a_{\vec{k}} \phi_k(\eta) e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{x}} + \text{h.c.} \right],
\end{equation}
with the mode functions satisfying the standard FRW equation. The
field is prepared in the Bunch--Davies vacuum at early times.

\subsection{Detector}

The observer is modelled as a harmonic oscillator of frequency
$\omega_0$ with canonical quadratures $R_O = (x_O, p_O)$ obeying
$[x_O, p_O] = i$ ($\hbar = 1$). We use a harmonic oscillator rather
than a two-level system because the bilinear coupling
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm int} = g\, x_O \, \phi(\vec{x}_O, \eta)
\end{equation}
preserves Gaussianity of the joint state, reducing the entire problem
to matrix algebra on covariance matrices. A two-level detector
coupled through $\sigma_x$ would break Gaussianity and would in
addition be subject to the bound $I(O{:}\phi) \le \ln 2$, which
truncates the analysis prematurely.

\subsection{Covariance matrix formalism}

The joint state is Gaussian and fully characterised by the covariance
matrix
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle \{R_i - \langle R_i \rangle, R_j - \langle R_j \rangle\} \rangle,
\end{equation}
with $R = (x_O, p_O, x_1, p_1, \dots, x_N, p_N)$ for $N$ field modes
retained. The symplectic eigenvalue $\nu_m \ge 1/2$ of each
$2\times 2$ block determines the von Neumann entropy
\begin{equation}
S(\nu) = (\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}) \ln(\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}) - (\nu - \tfrac{1}{2}) \ln(\nu - \tfrac{1}{2}).
\end{equation}
The information mutual between the detector and the field is
\begin{equation}
I(O{:}\phi) = S(\sigma_O) + S(\sigma_\phi) - S(\sigma_{\rm total}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quantum Fisher information}

The Fisher information of the detector state with respect to a time
parameter $\lambda$ is, for a Gaussian state,
\begin{equation}
F_Q^{(\lambda)} = \tfrac{1}{2} (\partial_\lambda \sigma)^T (\sigma \otimes \sigma - \tfrac{1}{4} \Omega \otimes \Omega)^{-1} (\partial_\lambda \sigma),
\label{eq:FQ}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is the symplectic form and the derivative is taken
with respect to $\lambda$. The Cram\'er--Rao bound then gives the
resolution limit
\begin{equation}
\Delta \lambda \ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{F_Q^{(\lambda)}}}.
\end{equation}

Two distinct time parameters are physically relevant:
\begin{itemize}
\item the detector's proper time $\tau_O$, and
\item the cosmic (conformal or scale-factor) time $\eta$ or $t$.
\end{itemize}
For an expanding universe, $dt = a\, d\eta$, so
$F_Q^{(t)} = F_Q^{(\eta)}/a^2$. Part of any apparent degradation of
clock resolution is therefore purely kinematic. Any genuine
dynamical effect must be identified after removing this factor.

\section{Unruh--DeWitt Consistency Check}
\label{sec:kms}

Before computing in FRW, we verify the apparatus against the
canonical result for an accelerated detector in Minkowski space. The
Wightman function along a uniformly accelerated trajectory in $1+1$
dimensions is
\begin{equation}
W(\Delta\tau) = -\frac{1}{4\pi} \ln\!\left[ -\frac{4}{a^2} \sinh^2\!\left( \frac{a\,\Delta\tau}{2} \right) - i\varepsilon \right].
\end{equation}
The KMS condition for a thermal state at inverse temperature
$\beta = 2\pi/a$ requires
\begin{equation}
W(\Delta\tau - i\beta) = W(-\Delta\tau).
\end{equation}
We test this identity numerically for
$a \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ and $\tau \in [0.01, 5.0]$. The results are
reported in Table~\ref{tab:kms}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
$a$ & $T = a/2\pi$ & $\max |\Delta W / W|$ \\
\midrule
$0.5$ & $0.0796$ & $1.0 \times 10^{-14}$ \\
$1.0$ & $0.1592$ & $5.0 \times 10^{-15}$ \\
$2.0$ & $0.3183$ & $2.6 \times 10^{-15}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{KMS condition test. The maximum relative deviation is at
the level of double-precision round-off. The apparatus is correct.}
\label{tab:kms}
\end{table}

The KMS test passes at machine precision. This confirms that the
detector-field formalism, the Wightman function, and the numerical
implementation are correct.

\section{Results in a Finite Truncation}
\label{sec:finiteN}

\subsection{Setup}

We take $N$ field modes with comoving momenta
$k_j = 2\pi j/L$, $j = 1, \dots, N$, on a comoving box of size
$L = 2\pi$. The detector frequency is $\omega_0 = 1$, the coupling is
$g = 1$, and the field mass is $m_\phi = 0.25$. The conformal time
runs from $\eta_i = 2$ to $\eta_f = 7$, corresponding to
$a \in [4, 49]$ in the matter-dominated background $a(\eta) = \eta^2$.
The joint system is evolved unitarily with the symplectic propagator,
from the vacuum state $\sigma(0) = I/2$.

\subsection{Information mutual and its convergence}

The maximum information mutual between detector and field during the
evolution is reported in Table~\ref{tab:Imax}.

\begin{table}[h]

\midrule
$5$  & $0.5252$ & $0.236$ & $-0.04$ \\
$10$ & $0.5269$ & $0.249$ & $+0.42$ \\
$20$ & $0.5277$ & $0.255$ & $-1.02$ \\
$40$ & $0.5283$ & $0.259$ & $-1.49$ \\
$80$ & $0.5285$ & $0.261$ & $-0.66$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Convergence of observables with the number $N$ of retained

\label{tab:Imax}
\end{table}

Two facts emerge:

\begin{itemize}
\item $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ converges monotonically to approximately
$0.26$ nats as $N$ increases. This is a stable, well-defined
observable of the model.

\item The exponent $\alpha$ fitted to the envelope of $F_Q^{(\eta)}$ in
the window $a \in [15, 35]$ does not converge. It changes sign between
successive values of $N$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation of the non-convergence of $\alpha$}

The non-convergence of $\alpha$ is not a numerical artefact. It is a
physical consequence of the finite-dimensional, unitary dynamics of
the model: the joint system has $2(N+1)$ degrees of freedom, its
spectrum is discrete, and unitary evolution produces quasi-periodic
orbits, not monotonic decay. The apparent ``decay'' of $F_Q^{(\eta)}$
in any finite window is an interference pattern, not a genuine
power-law. Any genuine decay requires a continuum limit.

\section{A Static-Bath Model: Quantum Brownian Motion}
\label{sec:QBM}

The simplest continuum model is to replace the discrete set of $N$
modes by a fixed Ohmic spectral density. This is \emph{not} derived
from the Wightman function of a field in FRW; it is a phenomenological
closure, equivalent to coupling the detector to an external bath at
fixed temperature $T_{\rm eff}$.

\subsection{Spectral density and master equation}

We adopt an Ohmic spectral density with exponential ultraviolet
cutoff,
\begin{equation}
J(\omega) = \eta\,\omega\,e^{-\omega/\Lambda},
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is a dimensionless coupling constant and $\Lambda$ is
the cutoff frequency. In the Markovian regime, the reduced dynamics
of the detector is described by the quantum Brownian motion master
equation in the high-temperature limit,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\eta} = M\sigma + \sigma M^T + N,
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
with drift matrix
\begin{equation}
M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1/m \\ -m\omega_0^2 & -\gamma/m \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and quantum noise matrix
\begin{equation}
N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & m\gamma\omega_0\coth\!\left(\frac{\omega_0}{2T_{\rm eff}}\right) \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
The damping coefficient is $\gamma = \pi\eta/2$. The model relaxes to
$\nu_\infty = \frac{1}{2}\coth(\omega_0/2T_{\rm eff})$, and the
symplectic eigenvalue decays with rate $\Gamma = \gamma = \pi\eta/2$.
This is mathematically exact for the Ohmic bath.

\section{Exact FRW Treatment: Bath Dilution and Memory}
\label{sec:FRW}

The correct way to obtain the damping kernel for a Gaussian detector
coupled to a scalar field in FRW is to compute it from the Wightman
function of the field.

\subsection{Wightman function and damping kernel}

For a massless scalar field in a spatially flat FRW universe with
$a(\eta) = \eta^n$ and in the Bunch--Davies vacuum, the rescaled mode
functions are
\begin{equation}
u_k(\eta) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi|\eta|}{4}}\,H_\nu^{(2)}(k|\eta|),
\qquad \nu = \left|n - \tfrac{1}{2}\right|,
\end{equation}
and the physical field is $\phi_k = u_k / a(\eta)$. We define the
Wightman function as
\begin{equation}
W(\eta, \eta') = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\,\frac{1}{a(\eta)a(\eta')}
\int_0^\infty dk\,k^2\,u_k(\eta)\,u_k^*(\eta').
\end{equation}
The damping kernel that enters the non-Markovian equation of motion
for the detector is
\begin{equation}
\gamma(\eta, \eta') = 2 g^2\,{\rm Im}\,W(\eta, \eta')\,\theta(\eta - \eta').
\label{eq:gammaFRW}
\end{equation}
The effective damping rate at time $\eta$ is
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\rm eff}(\eta) = \int_0^{\eta - \eta_{\min}} d\tau\,
\gamma(\eta, \eta - \tau)\cos(\omega_0 \tau),
\label{eq:gammaeff}
\end{equation}
with $\eta_{\min} \sim 1/\omega_0$.

\subsection{Analytic cases and sign convention}
\label{sec:dS_convention}

In two cases, closed-form expressions are available. For radiation
($n=1$), $u_k'' + k^2 u_k = 0$ (since $a''/a = 0$), and with the
Bunch--Davies convention $u_k \sim e^{-ik\eta}$ for $k|\eta| \gg 1$,
\begin{equation}
{\rm Im}\,W_{\rm conf}(\eta,\eta') =
-\frac{1}{4\pi^2 a(\eta)a(\eta')}\,
\frac{2\Delta\varepsilon}{(\Delta^2 + \varepsilon^2)^2},
\qquad \Delta = \eta - \eta'.
\label{eq:Wconf}
\end{equation}
The negative sign follows from $\mathrm{Im}[e^{-ik\eta}e^{+ik\eta'}]
= -\sin(k\Delta)$ for $\Delta > 0$. The corresponding $\gamma_{\rm
eff}$ is negative, which in the convention of \eqref{eq:gammaeff}
corresponds to positive damping. This is exact and has no infrared
divergence.

For de Sitter minimal ($n=-1$), we use the expanding branch $\eta < 0$
with scale factor $a(\eta) = -1/(H\eta)$. The Bunch-Davies vacuum
corresponds to the positive-frequency solution $\sim e^{-ik\eta}$ in
the far past. With this convention, the analytic expression is
\begin{equation}
{\rm Im}\,W_{\rm dS}^{\rm bare} =
-\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\left[\frac{2\eta\eta'\varepsilon\Delta}{(\Delta^2+\varepsilon^2)^2}
+ \arctan\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon}\right)
- \frac{\Delta\varepsilon}{\Delta^2 + \varepsilon^2}\right].
\label{eq:WdS}
\end{equation}
The bare value is negative for $\Delta > 0$ (anti-damping). The sign
is fixed by the Bunch-Davies choice of $H^{(2)}$; it is not an
artefact of the regulator. The $\arctan$ term dominates at late times
and is the infrared remnant.

For matter ($n=2$), the $k$-integral is computed numerically with a
UV regulator. In all cases, we have verified that the qualitative
behaviour (decay, sign change) is robust against the choice of
regulator, as discussed next.

\subsection{Robustness against UV regulators}
\label{sec:robustness}

The specific numerical values of $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ depend on the UV
regulator $\varepsilon$ and on the numerical integration parameters.
To verify that the qualitative conclusions do not depend on this
choice, we have computed $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ using two smooth
regulators: (i) exponential $e^{-\varepsilon k}$ and (ii) Gaussian
$e^{-\varepsilon^2 k^2}$.

A third regulator (sharp step at $k_{\max}$) was tested during
development but excluded because it introduces Gibbs oscillations in
the oscillatory $k$-integral, rendering the result numerically
unstable. The oscillatory factor $u_k u_k^*$ combined with a
discontinuous cutoff produces spurious high-frequency modes that
dominate the $\tau$-integration. This is a known numerical artefact,
not a physical effect.

The results for the two smooth regulators are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:robustness}. Two features are robust:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Robust: global decay.} In all backgrounds and for both
regulators, $|\gamma_{\rm eff}|$ decreases with $\eta$ and approaches
zero at late times. The static-bath prediction $\Gamma = \pi g^2/2$
is not reproduced.

\item \textbf{Robust: failure of Markovian dissipation.} In matter,
$\gamma_{\rm eff}$ changes sign for both regulators, indicating
memory effects (information returns to the detector from the field via
particle creation). A Markovian dissipation term, which is by
construction positive, cannot reproduce this.
\end{itemize}

We have computed $\gamma_{\rm eff}(\eta)$ for the three backgrounds.
The specific numerical values of $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ depend on the
UV regulator, on the infrared subtraction scheme, and on the numerical
integration parameters. They are therefore \emph{not} quoted here as
physical predictions. What is robust across all choices is the
qualitative behaviour, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:FRW}.

Three features are robust:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $|\gamma_{\rm eff}|$ decreases with $\eta$ rather than approaching
a constant. The static-bath prediction $\Gamma = \pi g^2/2$ is not
reproduced.
\item In the matter regime, $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ exhibits sign-changing
oscillations, indicating memory effects (information returns to
the detector from the field via particle creation). A Markovian
dissipation term, which is by construction positive, cannot reproduce
this.
\item In de Sitter, the massless minimally coupled field has an
infrared divergence, but in all smooth regularization schemes
$\gamma_{\rm eff}$ decays as $\eta \to 0^-$ rather than approaching a
constant.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Comparison and Cosmological Implications}
\label{sec:cosmo}

The two continuum models give qualitatively different answers: the
static bath gives exponential relaxation with $\Gamma = \pi\eta/2$
independent of $a(\eta)$, while the FRW field gives suppression with
the expansion plus oscillatory memory effects. Only the second
corresponds to a scalar field in an expanding universe.

\subsection{Temporal resolution in FRW}
\label{sec:resolution_bound}

In the FRW case, because $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ decays, the detector does
not lose coherence at a constant rate. The bound on temporal
resolution is therefore \emph{not} of the form
$\Delta\tau \gtrsim (2/\pi\eta)(1+z)^{-1}$ obtained from the
static-bath model. It weakens with time, becoming trivial at late
times. The informational bound $\Delta\tau \gtrsim
T_{\rm total}\,e^{-I_{\max}/2}$ discussed in the literature of
autonomous clocks \citep{Erker2017,Woods2019} is a comparative
constraint: with $I_{\max} \approx 0.26$ nats it gives a weak
numerical bound ($\Delta\tau/T_{\rm total} \gtrsim 0.88$), not a sharp
prediction.

\subsection{Reinterpretation of the UAT intuition}

The original UAT intuition---that the observer's temporal resolution
is bounded by its informational connection to the field---survives
the correction. What changes is the specific form of the bound: in
FRW, the connection weakens with the expansion, so the bound weakens
too. At early times ($z \gtrsim 1$), the detector is strongly coupled
to the field; at late times, it is effectively decoupled.

\section{What Is Not Claimed}
\label{sec:honesty}

For clarity, this paper does not claim:

\begin{enumerate}
\item That time runs at different rates for different observers.
The observable is clock resolution, not clock rate.

\item That the metric is modified, or that any cosmological
parameter is altered. The background is standard $\Lambda$CDM.

\item That the additive formalism of the original UAT is recoverable.
It is not. The correct formalism is the modular one.

\item That a power-law decay of clock resolution exists in
finite-dimensional unitary systems. It does not.

\item That the relaxation rate $\Gamma = \pi\eta/2$ of
Section~\ref{sec:QBM} describes a detector coupled to a cosmological
field. It does not. It describes a detector coupled to a static
external bath.

\item That the effective damping rate in FRW is constant. It is not;
it decays with the expansion and exhibits oscillatory memory
corrections.

\item That the first failure of the Markovian approximation in
curved spacetime is reported here. It is not. That failure is
established in the literature \citep{HsiangHu2021,Waghmare2025}. What
is new is the 3+1D Gaussian calculation with a full expansion history,
the explicit comparison with the Ohmic closure, and the framing via
modular information measures.

\item That UAT precedes or anticipates the modular program of
\citet{Chen2024, DeVuyst2025}. It does not. The convergence is
independent and parallel; see Section~\ref{sec:relation}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

The extensions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Full non-Markovian treatment with analytic regulators.}
The numerical computation of $\gamma_{\rm eff}(\eta)$ uses a sharp
UV cutoff. An analytic treatment with the correct distributional
prescription would isolate the physical damping from the Lamb shift.
\item \textbf{Massive field and conformal coupling.} For $m_\phi \neq
0$ or nonminimal coupling $\xi \neq 1/6$, the mode functions have a
different IR behaviour. In particular, the Gibbons--Hawking
temperature emerges for $m_\phi \sim H$.
\item \textbf{Full $\Lambda$CDM background with era transitions.}
The present calculation uses power-law $a(\eta)$. The full
$\Lambda$CDM case with radiation--matter and matter--dark-energy
transitions could show whether the era boundaries leave a signature
in $\gamma_{\rm eff}$.
\item \textbf{Relation to the crossed-product algebra and
quantum-reference-frame formulations}
\citep{Chandrasekaran2022, Chen2026, DeVuyst2025}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

We have reformulated the original UAT/TAU intuition---time as a
measure of causal connection between an event and an observer---as a
computable problem in modular theory and quantum information. The
reformulation yields:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{KMS verification.} The Unruh--DeWitt detector in
Minkowski space satisfies the KMS condition at machine precision.
\item \textbf{Information mutual.} $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ converges
with $N$ to $I_{\max} \approx 0.26$ nats. The power-law exponent
$\alpha$ of the Fisher-information decay does not converge with $N$;
the earlier value $\alpha \approx 0.72$ was an artefact.
\item \textbf{The Markovian approximation fails in FRW.} A static
Ohmic bath gives $\Gamma = \pi\eta/2$, but the exact Wightman function
of a scalar field in FRW gives a $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ that is not
constant, does not approach $\pi\eta/2$, and exhibits sign-changing
oscillations (memory effects) in the matter and dark-energy regimes.
The global decay is robust against the choice of smooth UV regulator.
\end{enumerate}

The comparison between the two continuum models is itself a
methodological result: \emph{phenomenological closures of the
continuum limit must be derived from the exact Wightman function of
the field, not assumed.} The Ohmic bath gives the right answer for
the wrong system.
